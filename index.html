What is Cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying is the use of digital technology — including social media, messaging apps, online games, and email — to repeatedly harass, threaten, embarrass, or target another person.

Unlike traditional bullying, cyberbullying can follow victims everywhere through their devices and can be anonymous, making it especially damaging.

Key Facts:
  • Over 36% of young people have experienced cyberbullying online.
  • Victims are 2–9 times more likely to consider self-harm.
  • Only 1 in 10 victims tell a parent or trusted adult.

Types of Cyberbullying

Harassment
Sending offensive, rude, and insulting messages repeatedly.
Impersonation
Pretending to be someone else online to damage their reputation.
Exclusion
Intentionally leaving someone out of online groups or activities.
Doxing
Publicly sharing private or personal information without consent.
Trolling
Making inflammatory comments to provoke emotional responses.
Sexting/Image Abuse
Sharing intimate images without consent.

Effects of Cyberbullying

Emotional Effects

  • Increased anxiety and depression
  • Feelings of isolation and loneliness
  • Low self-esteem and self-worth
  • Fear of using technology or going online

Academic Effects

  • Declining school performance
  • Reluctance to attend school
  • Difficulty concentrating in class
  • Dropping extracurricular activities

Social Effects

  • Withdrawal from friends and family
  • Loss of trust in relationships
  • Avoidance of social situations
  • Difficulty forming new friendships

How to Stay Safe on Social Media

01
Protect your privacy

Set your profiles to private and think before you share personal information.

02
Be selective with connections

Only accept friend or follow requests from people you know in real life.

03
Use strong passwords

Create unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication on all accounts.

04
Block and report bullies

Use platform tools to block harassers and report abusive behavior immediately.

05
Think before you post

Ask yourself: "Would I say this to someone's face?" if not, don't post it.

06
Save evidence

Screenshot and save any harmful messages before blocking or deleting.

How to Help Someone Experiencing Cyberbullying

Listen Without Judgment

Let them share their experience. Don't minimize or blame. Just be there.

Believe and Validate Them

Tell them you believe them and that what's happening is not okay.

Help Them Report

Help document evidence and report to the platform, school, or authorities.

Provide Emotional Support

Check in regularly, include them socially, and remind them they're valued.

Remember: Never share or forward bullying content — this spreads harm further. Be an upstander, not a bystander!
